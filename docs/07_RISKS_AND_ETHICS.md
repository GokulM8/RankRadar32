# 07 — Risks and Ethics

| # | Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation in the PoC | Further mitigation for production |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scaled content abuse.** Using gap lists to mass-produce thin articles. Google's spam policies name scaled content abuse (many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings, with or without automation) as a violation. | Medium / high | RankRadar outputs briefs, never articles, and has no publishing integration (ADR-006). Briefs say they are for human writers. | Keep the "brief, not article" boundary in the product. Cap briefs per client per month to what writers can genuinely produce. Track quality (time on page, engagement), not just volume. |
| 2 | **Copyright and plagiarism.** Briefs or articles reproducing competitor text. | Medium / high | The LLM sees only short excerpts and is told not to copy them. **In code**, any brief sharing an 8-word sequence with those excerpts is rejected, retried once with feedback and otherwise replaced by a template brief. This check exists because the prompt alone was not enough: in a pre-release run, 1 of 10 briefs reused competitor sentences almost verbatim. Full competitor text stays out of git; committed outputs hold short excerpts only, for analysis. | Run a plagiarism check on the final article as well as the brief. Keep excerpts short in any client-facing report. |
| 3 | **Crawling etiquette and terms.** Overloading sites or crawling against the owner's wishes. | Low / medium | robots.txt checked with my User-Agent (RFC 9309 rules, including "unreachable means disallowed"), a 1.5 s delay, a 60-post cap, public pages only and an identifiable User-Agent. Automely was skipped rather than worked around. | Respect `Crawl-delay`, cache with `ETag`/`Last-Modified`, check each site's terms of service, provide a contact URL in the User-Agent and honour opt-out requests. |
| 4 | **LLM hallucination.** Invented statistics, fake case studies or wrong facts in briefs. | Medium / medium | Grounding on excerpts only; instructions not to invent numbers, names or quotes; reference URLs, the internal link and the score are filled by code, not the model; schema validation with one retry and a template fallback; every brief records `source`, `model`, attempts and validation failures. | Human review of every brief before it reaches a writer (it is a brief, so this is cheap). Add a numeric-claim detector that flags any number not present in the excerpts. |
| 5 | **Subjective score weights.** The ranking reflects my judgement, not measured outcomes. | High / medium | Weights in config, adjustable live in the dashboard, a plain-language reason per row, a ±0.1 sensitivity check, and an honest comparison with a naive baseline (in which RankRadar did not win; see `06`). | Learn the weights from outcomes (Search Console results of published briefs) with client-grouped validation (`04` §8). |
| 6 | **Misreading the score as a ranking prediction.** Clients reading "83/100" as "83% chance to rank". | Medium / medium | The UI, docs and README say "opportunity score for prioritisation, not a ranking prediction". No traffic or ranking promises anywhere. | Show components rather than a single number in client reports, and add keyword volume and difficulty so the score is anchored to demand. |
| 7 | **Freshness.** Competitor blogs change; gaps close or open. | High / low | Every run is timestamped (`run_at`), and HTML is cached with fetch times. | Scheduled re-crawls and a "changed since last run" view. |
| 8 | **Evaluation bias.** Labels from one AI-assisted labelling pass (31 rows) that could see the scores. | High / medium | The method and its limits are documented in `06`; I report precision with and without low-confidence labels. | Two independent human labellers with agreement measured (Cohen's kappa), larger samples, and online evaluation on real traffic. |
| 9 | **Client data privacy.** Production would hold clients' Search Console data. | Medium / high | Not applicable to the PoC (public data only). Secrets live only in `.env`, which is git-ignored, and `.env.example` documents the variables. | OAuth with read-only Search Console scopes, per-client isolation (separate Postgres schemas and Pinecone namespaces), encryption at rest, data processing agreements, and no client data sent to an LLM provider without consent. Align with India's DPDP Act. |
| 10 | **Third-party API dependence.** Model ids change, free tiers throttle. | High / low | The LLM is optional (`--no-llm` gives keyword labels and template briefs); the model id is in config; exponential backoff on 429 and transient errors. The planned Nemotron model had already been delisted when I built this (ADR-007). | Pin model versions where the provider allows it, monitor for deprecations, and keep the fallback path tested in CI. |
| 11 | **Competitor self-promotion read as a "gap".** Clusters about a competitor's own products or events. | Medium / low | Site names are removed from keywords; the hand-label evaluation checks for this. In the final ranking, the two clearest self-promotion clusters rank 11th and 17th, outside the top 10. | Detect clusters dominated by a competitor's brand and product names and down-weight them. This needs validating first: in `06`, a simple self-mention filter would also have removed real gaps. |
